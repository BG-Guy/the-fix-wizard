Product Research

We tested 8 interior paint brands sold at New Jersey stores — 3 of them lose their finish within 18 months on NJ walls

New Jersey's climate is harder on paint than most homeowners realize. Humidity from the coast, freeze-thaw cycles in North Jersey winters, and the high moisture content in homes with poor ventilation all accelerate paint failure. We tested the eight most commonly purchased interior paint brands at NJ hardware stores across Bergen, Middlesex, and Morris counties — tracking sheen retention, washability, and adhesion over 18 months on actual NJ walls.

The three that showed early failure — visible sheen loss, chalking, and poor washability — were all budget-tier paints priced at $28–$35 per gallon. The irony: most NJ homeowners who buy them end up repainting within 2 years, effectively doubling the cost of the job. Premium paints in the $55–$75 range from Benjamin Moore, Sherwin-Williams, and similar brands showed dramatically better retention on NJ walls, particularly in high-traffic areas and rooms that face the coast or receive significant humidity variation.

The most important variable wasn't brand — it was sheen selection. Flat paint in a kitchen or bathroom fails almost universally in NJ homes within 18 months because flat finishes are not washable and absorb moisture. Using the correct sheen (eggshell or satin for living areas, semi-gloss for kitchens and baths) adds more longevity than any brand upgrade. We always discuss sheen selection as part of our free estimate — it's a conversation that adds years to your paint job at no extra cost.

Homeowner Survey

We spoke with 44 New Jersey homeowners who repainted rooms — most wasted money because of 3 prep mistakes

We gathered data from NJ homeowners who had repainting done — either DIY or by a contractor — within the previous two years. The most common complaint was consistent: the paint didn't look as good as expected, or it started peeling or chipping in specific areas within 12 months. In almost every case, the root cause wasn't the paint quality or even the application technique. It was prep work — the part of the job that no one talks about because it's boring and invisible.

Prep mistake number one: skipping the cleaning step. Walls in NJ kitchens, bathrooms, and high-traffic hallways accumulate grease, dust, and airborne residue that prevents proper adhesion. Paint applied over a dirty surface bonds to the surface layer, not the wall itself — and that layer peels. Prep mistake two: skipping primer on patched areas. Drywall compound is porous and absorbent; without primer, the paint soaks into the patch unevenly, creating visible "flashing" where the patch is visible through the topcoat. Prep mistake three: not allowing adequate dry time between coats — a problem that's worse in NJ's humid seasons when drying time is significantly extended.

The homeowners who reported the best outcomes — long-lasting results, clean lines, no peeling — all had one thing in common: they paid for proper prep, whether through a contractor who charged for it or by investing the time themselves. The prep phase on a standard NJ living room typically takes longer than the painting itself. Any contractor quoting you a very fast timeline on a large job is almost certainly skipping prep steps that will cost you money within two years.

Industry Finding

We found 5 painting shortcuts NJ contractors take that force homeowners to repaint within 2 years

New Jersey has no shortage of painting contractors, and competitive pricing pressure has created a race to the bottom in prep quality. We've been called in to repaint jobs done by other contractors within 24 months of completion more times than we can count — in homes from Hoboken to Cherry Hill. The shortcuts fall into five consistent patterns that are almost never disclosed to the homeowner upfront.

One: single-coat application with thin paint. Two coats is the standard for a reason — coverage, hide, and durability. One coat cut with water saves the contractor 30–40% in time and materials and shows its failure within 12–18 months. Two: no primer over new drywall or patched surfaces. Three: using interior paint on exterior surfaces because it's cheaper, or vice versa — both fail quickly in NJ's climate when used on the wrong substrate. Four: painting over mold or moisture-stained surfaces without treating the source — the mold returns through the paint in months. Five: skipping caulk on trim and corner joints, which causes gaps that collect dirt and eventually allow moisture infiltration in NJ's wet springs.

The only way to protect against these shortcuts is to either watch the work yourself, ask for a written scope that specifies coat count and primer usage, or hire someone with a reputation for doing it right. We put everything in writing before we start — coat count, primer specification, prep scope — so there's no ambiguity about what you're paying for. If a competing quote is dramatically cheaper, ask them what's included.
