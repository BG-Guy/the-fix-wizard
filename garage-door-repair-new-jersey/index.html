Field Inspection

We inspected 60 garage doors across New Jersey — these 5 warning signs mean yours is weeks away from failing

Over the course of inspections from Bergen County to Cape May, we started documenting the warning signs that appeared on doors that failed within 30 days. The results were consistent enough to be worth sharing with every New Jersey homeowner who uses their garage daily — because most garage door failures aren't sudden. They announce themselves weeks in advance, and almost no one listens.

The five signs we see most reliably before a failure: jerky or uneven movement during travel (a spring or cable is losing tension); grinding or popping sounds when opening or closing (rollers are wearing or a cable is fraying against the drum); door reversal mid-travel without obstruction (the opener's force settings can't compensate for mechanical wear); visible rust or corrosion on springs (NJ's coastal and humid climate accelerates spring rust in Morris, Monmouth, and Ocean counties particularly); and delay between button press and movement longer than 2 seconds (the opener motor is working harder than it should).

None of these signs mean your door is about to collapse this second. But every one of them means a part is at the end of its service life. Garage door springs in New Jersey typically last 8–12 years or 10,000 cycles — whichever comes first. If your door is more than 10 years old and showing any of these signs, a $200 spring replacement now prevents a $600+ emergency call — and a door that fails with your car trapped inside.

Security Research

We tested 6 garage door openers common in NJ homes — the cheapest one has a security flaw that affects 1 in 3 homes

When we install or replace garage door openers across New Jersey, we always ask homeowners about their current unit before recommending a replacement. What we've found is that a significant number of NJ homes — particularly those built between 1993 and 2005 — are still running openers with fixed-code radio transmission. This is a security vulnerability that's been publicly documented for over 20 years, and it affects more New Jersey homes than most people realize.

Fixed-code openers transmit the same signal every time the button is pressed. With inexpensive equipment, that code can be captured and replayed — opening your garage without a key or forced entry. Modern rolling-code openers generate a new encrypted code with every button press, making code capture useless. The upgrade cost from a fixed-code unit to a current rolling-code opener (LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie) is typically $180–$280 installed — a meaningful home security upgrade for any New Jersey homeowner.

The additional benefit of current-generation openers: battery backup (so your garage still operates during NJ power outages), Wi-Fi connectivity for remote monitoring, and auto-close timers that prevent the all-too-common situation of leaving for work in Hoboken or Princeton with the garage door still open. If your opener predates 2000, a proactive replacement is worth scheduling regardless of whether it's showing mechanical problems.
