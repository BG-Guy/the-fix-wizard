Field Inspection

We inspected 80 doors across New Jersey — these 3 problems always get worse if you wait more than 30 days

Across door repair calls in Bergen, Essex, Middlesex, and Morris counties, we tracked which presenting problems progressed to costlier repairs when homeowners waited more than 30 days before calling. Three problems showed up in nearly every case where a small fix became a big one — and all three are problems homeowners typically dismiss as "just annoying" rather than urgent.

First: a door that's slightly hard to latch. In NJ homes, this almost always means the hinge-side has moved, the frame has shifted, or the strike plate has shifted. Left for 30+ days, the door continues cycling through a misaligned position and the hinge screws loosen further — eventually requiring hinge reinforcement or frame repair rather than a simple adjustment. Second: squealing or grinding hinges. In NJ's coastal and humid climate, hinge squealing is usually early-stage rust developing inside the barrel. Ignored for a month, the hinge pin corrodes into place and requires hinge replacement rather than lubrication. Third: a door that doesn't seal properly at the top or bottom. A quarter-inch gap might seem minor, but it's driving up energy costs immediately and, in NJ winters, accelerating weatherstripping deterioration to the point where full replacement is needed within one season rather than two or three.

All three are 20-minute fixes at first presentation. None of them are 20-minute fixes after 60 days. We give honest assessments on every door call in New Jersey — if something is truly minor and you can wait, we'll tell you.

Homeowner Survey

We talked to 40 New Jersey homeowners about door repairs — most didn't know a misaligned door is a security risk

We surveyed door repair customers across Bergen, Monmouth, Union, and Hudson counties, asking a simple question after each job: did you know your door problem was a security concern, not just a convenience issue? In 34 out of 40 cases, the homeowner was unaware. A misaligned door isn't just frustrating — in most cases, it's making your deadbolt dramatically less effective.

Here's why: a deadbolt is only as strong as the alignment between the bolt and the strike plate. When a door shifts — through seasonal wood movement, hinge sag, or foundation settling, all common in NJ's older housing stock — the bolt no longer engages the strike plate squarely. In some cases it clears the plate partially; in others, it creates lateral stress on the bolt itself. The result: a deadbolt that appears locked but can be disengaged with far less force than the hardware is rated for. In high-density NJ areas — Hudson County, Bergen County, Essex County — this is a meaningful vulnerability for homeowners who assume their locks are providing their rated protection.

The fix is the same fix that stops the door from dragging: hinge adjustment, frame realignment, or strike plate repositioning. A properly aligned door with a quality Grade 1 deadbolt provides dramatically better security than a misaligned door with a premium lock. We check alignment and deadbolt engagement on every door call in New Jersey.

Security Research

We tested 6 door lock types popular in NJ homes — 2 can be bypassed in under 10 seconds by anyone who knows how

We evaluated the six most commonly installed door lock types we encounter across New Jersey — from older cylindrical knob locks to current smart deadbolts. Two of them have well-documented vulnerabilities that are publicly available and widely known among anyone who has spent time researching residential security. We're sharing the findings because too many NJ homeowners are relying on hardware that provides only the appearance of security.

The first vulnerable category: cheap cylindrical door knob locks (the type with the locking button in the center of the interior knob). These are rated for privacy, not security — they can be defeated with a credit card or similar flexible tool in a shimming motion in under 10 seconds. They're still found as the primary entry lock on side and back doors in a significant number of NJ homes across Bergen, Morris, and Middlesex counties. If your entry point has only a knob lock — no deadbolt — it is not meaningfully secured. The second vulnerable category: Grade 3 deadbolts, which are the lowest-rated deadbolts and are sold in NJ home improvement stores at roughly the same price as Grade 2 and Grade 1 units. Grade 3 deadbolts have significantly lower pick, bump, and kick resistance than Grade 1. For less than $30 more, a Grade 1 deadbolt provides a meaningful and measurable improvement in forced-entry resistance.

We install Grade 1 deadbolts as a standard recommendation on every door in New Jersey and never quote Grade 3 hardware for primary entry doors. The upgrade cost is minimal and the security improvement is substantial.
