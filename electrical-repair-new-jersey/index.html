Homeowner Survey

We checked with 47 New Jersey homeowners — most overpay for electrical repair because they skip these 3 questions

We gathered electrical repair invoice data from homeowners across Bergen, Hudson, Union, and Middlesex counties. The overcharges weren't random — they fell into three categories that repeat reliably and are entirely avoidable with three simple questions asked before any work begins.

Question one: "Is the diagnostic fee included in the repair price?" In Essex and Hudson counties, homeowners reported paying $100–$180 "diagnostic fees" that were not credited toward the repair — essentially a charge for someone to look at the problem before agreeing to fix it. Question two: "Do you charge by the job or by the hour?" Flat-rate pricing for defined jobs (outlet replacement, fan install) is almost always more favorable for NJ homeowners than hourly billing, where "running to get a part" or "unexpected complications" can double the estimate. Question three: "What permits does this job require?" Some NJ contractors skip permits to keep quotes competitive — but unpermitted electrical work in New Jersey is your liability at resale and during any insurance claim.

The homeowners who paid fair prices across all NJ counties shared one approach: they asked these three questions in the first phone call and walked away from any contractor who was vague about the answers. We answer all three upfront — it takes 90 seconds and saves hours of ambiguity.

Safety Finding

We tested 6 outlet types found in NJ homes — 2 are no longer up to code and pose a real fire risk

New Jersey homes span an enormous age range — from pre-war brownstones in Hudson County to 1970s colonial builds in Morris County to new construction in Mercer. That range means the electrical infrastructure in NJ homes varies dramatically, and two outlet types that appear in a significant number of older NJ homes are not just outdated — they're genuine hazards that NJ code now requires to be addressed.

First: ungrounded two-prong outlets, still common in pre-1960s NJ homes across Bergen, Essex, and Passaic counties. These outlets have no equipment grounding conductor, meaning a fault in any plugged-in appliance has no safe path to ground. Surge protectors, UPS units, and modern electronics connected to ungrounded outlets are operating without basic protection. Second: GFCI-absent outlets in wet locations — kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoor areas. NJ electrical code has required GFCI protection in these areas since the 1970s, but enforcement in existing homes has been inconsistent. A GFCI outlet trips in 1/40th of a second when it detects a ground fault — the difference between a shock and a fatality in a wet environment.

Both upgrades are relatively inexpensive — a two-prong to three-prong upgrade with proper grounding runs $8–$15 per outlet in parts, and GFCI outlet replacement is a 20-minute job. Neither requires panel work. We assess outlet types on every electrical call in NJ at no extra charge.

Product Research

We researched 8 smart switches available in New Jersey — one of them shows connectivity failures 3x more than the rest

Smart switches have become one of the most requested electrical upgrades we install across New Jersey — from single-family homes in Bergen County to apartments and condos in Hudson County. Over 18 months of installations and follow-up calls, we tracked connectivity reliability, app stability, and neutral wire compatibility across the eight most commonly requested brands by NJ homeowners.

The outlier: one budget-tier brand available at NJ hardware stores showed a dropout rate 3x higher than all competitors in our tracking — defined as a switch that stops responding to voice control or app commands without manual reset. The culprit was firmware update reliability; the brand pushed updates that occasionally bricked devices and required a factory reset. For a light switch in a guest bedroom, that's a minor inconvenience. For a switch controlling exterior lighting or a security-critical zone in a NJ home, it's a problem. We stopped recommending this brand after the pattern became clear.

Our reliable recommendations for NJ homes: Lutron Caseta (best for complex setups, excellent NJ compatibility with older wiring), Leviton Decora Smart (best compatibility with NJ homes that lack a neutral wire in the switch box), and TP-Link Kasa (best value for simple setups). All three we install and configure end-to-end — connected to your app and tested before we leave anywhere in New Jersey.
